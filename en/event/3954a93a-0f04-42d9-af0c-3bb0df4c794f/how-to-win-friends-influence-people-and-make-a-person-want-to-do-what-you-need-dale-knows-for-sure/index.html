How to win friends, influence people, and make someone want to act the way you need them to? — Dale Carnegie knows exactly how😅 He built an entire philosophy of communication on this. Everett Shostrom wrote "Anti-Carnegie" and asked a practically opposite question: "Can you stop managing people in order to be in a relationship with them?" Many who work with people know how to tune communication to get the desired reaction from another person while making them feel good. In sales, it's an absolute must 😅 We go to trainings to learn how to communicate, meet people, build positioning, be likable, and be able to "sell" ourselves: to a client, a customer, a potential partner — including a romantic one. And honestly, that's great! But what happens when we bring this into the space where we want closeness, honesty, and genuine connection? Maybe relationships can actually be built from the very beginning not according to Dale Carnegie? This Thursday we're discussing: 🤩 "Anti-Carnegie or the Manipulative Person" (Everett Shostrom) This time a slightly different format: one book — one meeting.
